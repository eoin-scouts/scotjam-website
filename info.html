Info for leaders and parents

This page is for parents, carers, leaders and groups. It covers the practical things you need before and during ScotJam. Some details for 2027 are still to be confirmed, and we'll update this page as soon as we can. If your young person already has a place, please include your ScotJam reference in any email to us.

Travel and arrival

ScotJam takes place at Scout Adventures Lochgoilhead, Lochgoilhead, Cairndow, PA24 8AQ. If the 2027 venue is different, we will update this page. More details coming soon. The centre sits close to Loch Goil and the Argyll Forest Park.

Scout Adventures Lochgoilhead on Google Maps

The road from the Rest and Be Thankful (A83) down to Lochgoilhead is a single-track road. On arrival you may be directed to park in the Lochgoilhead village car park, then make the short walk to the centre. There is also a short walk from the drop-off point to the sleeping accommodation, so bags should be manageable.

In recent years, families travelling by car have had a set arrival window on the first day and a collection window on the last day. Those times differ by section. Arrival and collection times for each 2027 event will be confirmed here. More details coming soon.

Coach or minibus travel has often been offered from key places across Scotland, subject to demand and usually at an extra cost. In 2026, coaches for Cubs and Scouts were planned from Aberdeen, Dundee, Perth, Stirling, Edinburgh and Glasgow. Explorers were offered minibus travel from Edinburgh, Stirling and Glasgow, with a link from a scheduled coach service for some families further north. Pickup points, times and prices are confirmed with booked families, and they can change with demand.

Whether transport will be offered in 2027, from which places, and how families opt in, will be confirmed here. More details coming soon. Please do not treat the 2026 pickup lists as a promise for 2027.

Accommodation and meals

Accommodation is indoor, and meals are provided for those staying on site. Scout Adventures Lochgoilhead has dorms and lodges. We aim to place participants from the same group and similar ages together in single-gender rooms. Room allocations are shared with families in due course.

As a guide to the buildings (from the previous website):

  • Dorms 1 and 4: two 10-bed rooms and a 2-bed leader room
  • Dorms 2 and 3: two 8-bed rooms, one 4-bed room, and a 4-bed leader room
  • Lodges 1, 2 and 3: two 4-bed rooms and three 2-bed rooms

Pillows, pillowcases and sheets are provided. Participants bring a sleeping bag, and may bring their own pillowcase and fitted sheet if they prefer. We'll confirm that this still applies in 2027. More details coming soon.

Allergies and dietary requirements (including medical and cultural diets) need to be told to us in advance, on the participant information form. We can cater for almost all requirements if we know in time. Please do not send snacks containing nuts or nut products. If your child needs to bring specific food for medical, allergy or dietary reasons, contact the ScotJam team first.

Kit list

The list below is a starting point, based on the public kit list from the previous website. Participants should help pack their own kit. Please avoid packing new or expensive clothes, as they may get damaged. Mark belongings with the young person's full name and Group. Pack into a rucksack, holdall or strong case. Young people may need more than one change of clothes in a day, depending on the programme and the weather.

Clothing

  • Uniform jumper or shirt. There is no need to bring a necker or woggle; participants have received a ScotJam necker on arrival. We'll confirm the 2027 necker arrangements. More details coming soon.
  • Polo shirts or T-shirts, including some long-sleeved
  • Fleece, hoodie or jumper (fleeces dry quickly and keep heat when wet; wool also works well when wet)
  • Trousers, joggers or leggings (not jeans; even if someone plans to wear shorts, they must bring trousers, because some activities need covered legs)
  • Shorts
  • Night clothes
  • Socks and underwear, with spares
  • Swimwear and clothes that can get wet, more than one set
  • Swim or sport shorts (for Scouts and Explorers, an old pair is useful if there is a burn run, to wear over a wetsuit)
  • Warm hat and gloves, and a sun hat
  • Waterproof jacket and trousers
  • At least three pairs of footwear: old trainers for water activities; trainers or strong shoes for land activities; walking boots. Crocs, sandals, sliders and flip-flops are not suitable for activities.
  • Bright or neon clothing if there is a final-night disco

Personal kit

  • Wash kit (no spray deodorants)
  • At least two towels, plus a hand towel
  • Sleeping bag
  • Day bag
  • Head midge net, and midge repellent (no DEET-based sprays, on that kit list)
  • Sun cream
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Hair bobbles, if needed
  • Glasses case, if needed
  • Personal medication, marked with name and dose frequency
  • Spending money, currently a suggested maximum of £10 to £15 in small notes. The 2027 limit will be confirmed here. More details coming soon.
  • A torch, and a cuddly toy if they would like one
  • Optional quiet activities: a book, cards or a small travel game

Please leave at home

  • Electronic devices, including phones and smart watches, unless they are needed for medical reasons and this has been agreed in advance on the participant information form. Whether any section may bring a phone in 2027 will be confirmed here. More details coming soon.
  • Knives

Good to know

The weather at Lochgoilhead can change quickly, even in summer. The centre has dry rooms for wet clothing. All specialist activity equipment is provided. Activities follow The Scout Association's safety rules. Organisers cannot accept responsibility for personal equipment, clothing and effects, and The Scout Association does not provide automatic insurance for those items.

Please label bags and important items, and help your child pack so they know what they have brought. Lost property has been kept at Scout Adventures Lochgoilhead for one month after the event, then donated to charity. We'll confirm that this still applies in 2027. More details coming soon.

Safety, wellbeing and behaviour

Safety and wellbeing sit at the heart of ScotJam, as they do across Scouts. Everyone has the right to a positive experience. Adult volunteers are experienced and have the checks and training required by the Scouts, including a PVG and safeguarding training.

If a young person is struggling with behaviour, a familiar adult will talk with them calmly, help them understand what needs to change, and may give a short break to reset. If it continues, the Event Leadership Team will be told, and parents or carers may be contacted. If behaviour is severe or unsafe, a young person may need to leave the event. In that case, parents or carers are responsible for transport, and recent events have not provided a refund for a behavioural dismissal. We'll confirm that this remains the approach in 2027. More details coming soon.

Many young people feel homesick at some point. The house or welfare team in their dorm or lodge will support them first. Families are contacted if needed, at the team's discretion. It helps if you do not promise that they can come home early if they don't like it, and if you tell us in advance what usually helps them. Help them understand they will not have routine contact with you during the event, and that they will have plenty to tell you afterwards.

Medication and participant information

We need information about your young person so we can meet their needs and contact you if we have to. This is collected on the participant information form, and includes:

  • Two emergency contacts, both reachable throughout the event, with at least one able to travel to the centre or to a medical setting if needed
  • Medical information and any medication they must bring (including inhalers they may not use often at home)
  • Dietary requirements
  • An optional participant profile, to help us understand how they respond to new situations and how we can support them

This information is stored securely and, on recent events, has been kept for two months after the event in case of queries, then securely destroyed. The 2027 retention period will be confirmed here. More details coming soon.

All medication except emergency medication must be handed to the volunteer team for safe storage and administration. It must be labelled with the participant's name and accommodation, with clear instructions. If they travel by bus, it should be given to the leader in charge at the pickup point. If they travel by car, it should be given to the house team at check-in. Emergency medication, such as adrenaline auto-injectors and inhalers, must stay with the young person (or their designated leader) at all times. A spare inhaler is helpful.

Digital devices

ScotJam is a time to be present, make friends and have a break from screens. There are also safety concerns about photos and videos, and limited storage and charging on site.

Please leave phones, smart watches and other devices at home, unless they are needed for a medical reason that we have agreed in advance. If you think some contact during the event may be necessary, talk to us beforehand so we can agree a plan. In an urgent situation, contact the event team and we will help with communication.

To help friendships last after the event, we have given each young person an address book so they can swap details if they wish. They may bring a note of a phone number or social account they are happy to share; the house team can keep this safe until the end of the event.

We post some updates on official ScotJam social channels during the event. We cannot promise that every young person will appear in a photograph.

Keeping in touch

If a question is not urgent, email scotjam@scouts.scot and include your ScotJam reference.

During the event, the team will only phone a family when they need to, usually about wellbeing or an important programme change. You may see a call from an unknown or withheld number. No news is good news: if you don't hear from us, things are running as planned. A call is not automatically an emergency.

On-event contact numbers, when they are issued, will be sent to booked families. They will not be published in full on this public page.
